Introduction

From May to August 2026, I worked as the IT Support Intern for TaylorMade Golf Canada, based out of the Vaughan, ON head office. Over four months, my role grew from learning how the Canada IT team supports the business day to day, into helping improve how parts of that business actually operate — through asset management, device upgrades, and a business process automation project that became the centerpiece of my term. This report walks through that journey, the results it produced, and what I took away from it.

TaylorMade Golf Canada

TaylorMade is a golf equipment manufacturer with a global presence and regional offices around the world, including the Canada head office in Vaughan, Ontario where I was based. I supported the Canada IT team, working alongside Alex Muriuki, Senior IT Analyst, on day-to-day operational support, asset management, and process improvement initiatives for the Canada business.

Goals: A Four-Month Journey

Rather than a fixed list of learning objectives, my goals for this term evolved in four phases as my understanding of the business deepened.

May — Learning

Shadowed tickets and got familiar with how IT operates at TaylorMade and how it supports teams across the business.

June — Execution

Moved into asset audits, inventory validation, and process documentation.

July — Scaling

Wrote SOPs and improved documentation meant to outlive my internship, and started exploring automation opportunities.

August — Delivering

Focused on larger initiatives: workflow automation, process optimization, and handoff preparation.

What started as learning how IT supports the business evolved into helping improve how the business operates — and that shift became the throughline for the rest of my term.

Job Description

As the IT Support Intern, I supported day-to-day Canada IT operations while also owning a set of standalone projects. Over four months, that meant:

  • Supporting 200+ of roughly 400 Canada IT tickets and 100+ direct user support interactions, working alongside Alex on operational support
  • Reviewing and validating 120+ hardware and software assets, correcting untracked devices, missing serial records, and ownership inconsistencies
  • Upgrading and validating 30+ devices to Windows 11 to reduce unsupported-software risk
  • Designing and building a Power Automate framework to automate approval workflows across the business
  • Scoping and prototyping an AI-powered self-service support assistant

Asset Audit & Windows 11 Upgrades

The asset audit produced value that outlasted the audit itself: identifying previously untracked devices, records missing serial information, and inconsistencies in ownership gave TaylorMade a more reliable asset register and stronger visibility into the Canada environment — something that will matter directly during upcoming office renovations and relocations. The Windows 11 upgrade work followed the same principle: beyond simply upgrading 30+ devices, I documented the process so future upgrade cycles can run more efficiently, leaving behind a more standardized, lower-risk environment.

Biggest Success: Process Automation

The project I'm most proud of focused on TaylorMade's approval request processes — Price Changes, Financial Credits, Payment Terms, Return for Credit, and several others. These business processes required review and approval before work could move forward, and at the time, most of that process relied on manual actions: requests launched manually, notifications sent manually, and users often following up themselves just to find out where a request stood.

That led me to a simple question: why was all of this still manual? What looked at first like a straightforward workflow turned out to be a much larger business process involving multiple stakeholders, years of history, and plenty of edge cases. I had no prior experience building business automation solutions coming into this internship — I learned Power Automate over the course of this project, and what began as a single workflow optimization expanded into a reusable framework supporting multiple processes, including Price Changes, Financial Credits, Payment Terms, and Product Movement requests.

The results were concrete: requests now begin processing automatically within seconds, and notifications, reminders, and status tracking are all automated rather than manually monitored. At roughly fifteen requests per month, that's about 180 requests per year that no longer require the same level of administrative follow-up. The goal was never to remove people from the process — it was to remove the repetitive work surrounding it.

What's Next: Self-Service Support

While supporting the service desk, I noticed many requests were for questions that already had documented answers — the challenge wasn't a lack of information, it was helping people find it quickly. To address that, I scoped and prototyped an AI-powered self-service support assistant designed to surface SOPs and knowledge base articles conversationally, and discussed the concept with the US team, who are exploring similar opportunities for production deployment.

Biggest Challenge

Interestingly, learning the technology wasn't the hard part — understanding a business process well enough to actually improve it was. The approval process I worked on had been in place for years, with multiple stakeholders, exceptions, manual workarounds, and evolving requirements. To improve it successfully, I had to spend real time understanding how the process actually worked before making any changes: working closely with stakeholders, testing, gathering feedback, and adapting the solution while still meeting project deadlines.

Skills and Learning

The most valuable lesson I learned at TaylorMade wasn't about Power Automate or IT support specifically — it was that the best solutions come from understanding the people, the process, and the business objective before jumping into the technology. Before this internship, I would have assumed technical knowledge was the most important part of solving a problem. Now I believe understanding the problem itself matters even more. Going forward — whether in IT, process improvement, project management, or software development — I plan to approach challenges by first understanding how people work, what the business actually needs, and whether technology is really the right solution before reaching for it.

Conclusions

Looking back on these four months, I'm proud of the projects, the improvements, and the results — the ticket support, the asset audit, the Windows 11 upgrades, and especially the automation framework that will keep saving time long after my internship ends. What I'm most proud of isn't really the volume of work completed; it's that many of these improvements continue creating value after the work itself is finished.

But what I'll remember most from this term are the people. The support, mentorship, patience, and humour made this experience not only valuable professionally, but genuinely enjoyable personally.

Acknowledgments

I'd like to thank Alex Muriuki and the rest of the Canada IT team, along with everyone I had the opportunity to work with throughout my internship, for their guidance, patience, and trust throughout this term.
